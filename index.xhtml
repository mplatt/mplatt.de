Moritz Platt

Designing critical infrastructure at Google. Researching resilient systems at King’s. Engineering technology that makes digital trust accessible to everyone.

Google Scholar ORCID LinkedIn Instagram

I hold a PhD in Computer Science from King’s College London, where I researched identity-based consensus protocols under the supervision of Prof. Peter McBurney. My academic path also includes degrees from TU Berlin and Birkbeck, University of London.

I currently serve as a Technology Manager at Google, a role I balance with periodic research collaborations alongside my colleagues. My work sits at the intersection of decentralised systems security and the broader challenge of creating fairness under adversarial conditions. What drives me is both the intellectual challenge and a sense of responsibility: how do we build infrastructure that remains secure and equitable even when incentives are misaligned, so that access and trust aren't reserved for the privileged few?

Beyond systems architecture, I place a high value on the human side of technology. I enjoy teaching and public speaking, viewing them not just as opportunities to present findings, but as challenges to translate complex concepts into clear, accessible narratives. I am particularly committed to mentoring the next generation of researchers and builders, as well as fostering an open discussion in the field.

I am based in Berlin, though my commitments frequently draw me to London. I protect my time fiercely, but I am never too busy for a truly exceptional connection. If you have an intriguing perspective to share over a great coffee in either city, please feel free to reach out.

Celebrating the completion of my doctorate at King’s College London.
